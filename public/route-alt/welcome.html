Before You Begin

When the Summit Is Closed, the Mountain Still Has More to Offer

The mountaintop amusement park doesn't run its night hours every evening. On the nights it doesn't, this route swaps the summit deck for something older: a ride on the Hozanji Line, a separate stretch of cable track that runs independently of the summit line, up to Hozanji — a working Buddhist temple that's been a major pilgrimage site since long before the amusement park existed. You'll still get the same skyline opener at City Hall and the same shrine-town finale in Ishikiri — just a different, quieter middle.

A Slower Kind of Night

Instead of rides and lights, tonight's middle stretch is a night view over Nara from a mountainside temple, followed by more time in Ishikiri's shrine town — through its upper and lower halls, past a street of fortune tellers that's been read by locals for generations, and finally to the quiet ritual of ohyakudo mairi, a hundred-times pilgrimage walked by visitors who came for reasons of their own.
